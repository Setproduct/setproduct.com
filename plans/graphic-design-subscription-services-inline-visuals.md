# Инлайн-визуалы для «8 best graphic design subscription services worth your money»

## Где читатель спотыкается

Самый сложный для понимания фрагмент — блок [«Turnaround times and active requests»](content/blog/graphic-design-subscription-services.mdx:93). Там в двух абзацах сжаты три разные модели пропускной способности: очередь, активные проекты и дневные часы. Дальше эти три модели раскиданы по всей таблице и по восьми карточкам сервисов, но ни разу не показаны рядом.

Почему это дорого читателю: почти все провайдеры пишут «unlimited requests», и на этом слове покупка обычно и заканчивается. На практике цена в $349 и цена в $1,199 отличаются не объёмом запросов, а тем, сколько работ реально движется одновременно. Без визуала читатель уходит с мыслью «unlimited значит unlimited» и выбирает план по headline price. Именно эту ошибку статья называет главной.

Три визуала ниже закрывают пробел с трёх сторон, и все три ставятся в один блок, чтобы текст перестал быть простынёй.

## Визуал 1 — «Три модели пропускной способности» (диаграмма Ганта)

**Куда вставить:** сразу после первого абзаца блока «Turnaround times and active requests», до фразы «That is why buyers should compare».

**Prompt:**

Create a clean editorial infographic on a strictly pure white background (#FFFFFF), no tint, no gradient, no texture, no border, no shadow behind the frame. Flat vector only, precise geometry, consistent two-pixel line weight, generous whitespace. No photorealism, no 3D, no lettering except the short labels listed below.

Title area at the top: the words "40 requests, 10 days, three plans" in a modern geometric sans, sentence case, dark charcoal, left aligned, one line.

Main body: a horizontal Gantt-style timeline, ten equal day columns marked 1 to 10 along the top in small grey numerals with thin vertical gridlines. Three stacked rows beneath, each row labelled on the far left in small charcoal type: "Queue, one task", "Three active projects", "Daily hours".

Row one: a single slender bar in soft violet runs from day 1 to day 3, then a second bar day 3 to day 5, then day 5 to day 7. Bars are separated by thin grey gaps and stacked sequentially, so only one bar is ever active. A small grey counter at the row end reads "3 done".

Row two: three parallel lanes inside the row, each carrying its own violet bar starting day 1, ending day 4, day 4, and day 5. Bars overlap vertically, clearly simultaneous. Counter reads "12 done".

Row three: a filled violet block spanning days 1 to 10 with a lighter violet band above it showing unused capacity on days 4 and 9. Counter reads "14 done".

Use exactly one accent colour, violet #7C4DFF, plus charcoal #19181B and two greys. Keep all three counters aligned in a right-hand column so the comparison reads as one glance.

Aspect ratio: 16:9.

Alt text: Three plans running the same 40 requests over ten days, showing queue, three active projects, and daily hours.

## Визуал 2 — «Куда уходят unlimited requests» (воронка)

**Prompt:**

Headline at the top left in a geometric sans, sentence case, charcoal: "Unlimited requests is not unlimited output". Under it, one grey subtitle line: "Where 40 monthly requests actually go".

To the right of the funnel, a slim vertical legend in three lines with small swatches: pale violet for submitted, lavender for in production, outlined violet for delivered.

Palette: charcoal #19181B, violet #7C4DFF, lavender #EDE7FF, two neutral greys. Everything sits on white without any frame, so the graphic floats in the article column.

Top headline, geometric sans, sentence case, charcoal, left aligned: "Price tells you what you pay, not what moves". Grey subtitle below: "Entry plan, published figures only".

Left half: a vertical bar chart. Four bars under a thin baseline, labelled below in small grey type: "Graphically", "Penji", "Duck", "Superside". Bar heights encode the published entry price: $349, $995, $1,199, $15,000. Values sit above each bar in charcoal numerals. Bars are outlined in violet with a very pale violet fill, evenly spaced, no shadows, no internal gradients. The shortest bar is drawn solid violet to anchor the eye.

Right half: a compact two-axis plot, both axes thin charcoal lines. Vertical axis labelled "Entry price, dollars" with four faint gridlines. Horizontal axis labelled "Concurrent work allowed", with three tick marks at 1, 3, and custom. Four groups of dots sit on the plot, one per service, positioned by its published price and its published concurrency. Graphically and Duck sit above the 1 tick, Penji above the 3 tick, Superside above the custom tick. Palette: charcoal, violet, pale violet, two greys, white. Generous whitespace, no decoration, no invented percentages.
