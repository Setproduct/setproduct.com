Most "guest posting opportunities" lists on the internet were assembled in 2019 and forgotten. Half the sites in those lists are dead. Another quarter stopped accepting submissions months ago. The rest ask $50 and publish anyone with a pulse, so the listed URL still "accepts" guest posts but the link passes nothing.

This list is different. We re-verified every site in the first week of September 2026. We clicked through every submission link while writing this article. We read the official guidelines pages on each site. We filtered out anything that looked like a private blog network, which we treat as a cluster of thin-content sites with no real author bios and the same set of footer links.

Below you'll find 35 active sites grouped by niche, sorted by domain rating (DR is Ahrefs' 0–100 backlink-strength score). Each row is tagged free, paid, or editorial-only. The first three tables cover editorial sites, then a short section lists free community platforms that anyone can post on.

## How this list was built

Five rules kept the list honest:

→ We tested every submission form in August 2026 and recorded which editors replied within 14 days. Sites that ignored three different pitches from different test accounts are flagged as having low reply rates.

→ DR data comes from Ahrefs, pulled on 2026-09-01. DR moves month to month, so treat the column as directional, not absolute.

→ Free or paid status comes from reading each site's official submission or contributor page, not from a third-party directory or a stale "Write For Us" listicle.

→ We excluded any site with obvious PBN signals from this list: thin content under 400 words per article, no author bios, or sitewide footer links to unrelated products from a single owner.

→ Where we run a paid program ourselves, the row in the tech and SaaS table reflects that with the same format as every other entry. Editors read every submission. The program does not auto-publish.

If a site didn't reply inside two weeks during the test phase, we marked it "verify link" in the Notes column. Treat those entries as a starting point for your own outreach, not a green light.

## Free guest posting sites

These platforms are about community publishing and audience building. They rarely move DR or pass ranking equity. A backlink from a personal Medium post carries a fraction of the weight that an editorial backlink passes. Pick them when the goal is brand, portfolio, and writing habit, not DR.

→ Dev.to: open community, indexed within hours, any dev topic

→ Medium personal publication: keep 100% of your audience, custom domain available

→ Hashnode: free developer blog host with a built-in network

→ HackerNoon free tier: separate from the paid tag program, less competitive

→ LinkedIn Articles: indexable, posts sit on your professional profile

→ Substack Notes: short-form posts that build a following before a paid newsletter

If your goal is a single backlink that moves a commercial page up the SERPs, none of these will deliver. If your goal is audience-building and protecting your IP, they will.

## How to actually get your pitch accepted

![The 7-step guest post pitch checklist as a circular flow diagram](/blog/assets/guest-posting-opportunities/pitch-checklist.webp "float-right")

❶ Read three recent posts on the site before pitching. Editors reject pitches that ignore what's already live in the last 60 days.

❷ Reference a specific article the editor published recently, then describe what you liked about it. Generic praise reads as spam and gets ignored.

❸ Send three working titles, not one. Editors prefer options over closed-ended asks. Three angles also reveal that you actually understand the site.

❹ Pitch a topic the site hasn't covered, not one they have. Duplicates get deleted without a reply, and editors remember that.

❺ Attach a Google Doc or live link of the best published piece you've shipped. Editors want proof that you can write and edit on deadline.

❻ Avoid pitching anything that competes with the host's product or service. A CRM post on a CRM vendor's blog is a hard no regardless of how good the draft is.

❼ Follow up exactly once, seven days later. Two follow-ups feels like pressure and burns the relationship. Zero follow-ups usually means your first email got buried.

## Free vs paid guest posts: which one moves rankings

Free platforms are mostly community publishing, not real guest posting. They build brand recall, add portfolio pieces, and create a writing habit. They don't typically move DR, and they rarely pass equity-grade link juice because most of them use nofollow tags or sit on low-DR outbound profiles.

Paid placements on editorial sites move rankings. They also move referral traffic if the host has actual readership. The trade-off here is budget, not morality, and a SaaS marketer with margins can usually justify one or two such placements per quarter.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best guest posting site in 2026?

There is no single "best" site. It depends on niche, audience, and the link equity you want to pass. For tech and SEO audiences, Smashing Magazine and A List Apart remain the highest-DR free options. For AI and ML, Towards Data Science and KDnuggets carry the most weight. For SaaS and growth, Buffer Blog is the most trusted. Match the site to your topic, and the rest follows. The full process is in [our guest post guidelines](https://www.setproduct.com/blog/submit-a-guest-post).

### How much does a guest post cost on a DR 40+ site?

Pricing varies widely. Mid-tier editorial placements in the DR 40 to 60 range, with full editorial review, run $200 to $400 per slot. Top-tier outlets in the DR 80+ tier often charge $400 to $1,000+ for sponsored placements, and many no longer accept cold pitches at any price. Free's editorial gate stays separate from the price tier above it. Always confirm current rates on the submission page before you budget, since pricing moves month to month.

### Are free guest posting sites worth it for SEO?

Free platforms such as Dev.to, Medium, and Hashnode build an audience and a portfolio. They rarely pass link equity that moves rankings, because most use nofollow tags or sit on low-DR outbound profiles. Use them for brand and IP, not for ranking a single commercial page on a tight deadline.

### How long does it take to get a guest post accepted?

Free editorial sites take two to six weeks on average, sometimes longer. Top-tier outlets like Smashing Magazine and A List Apart reply in roughly two weeks and queue publication for two months. Paid placements publish faster; 3-day turnaround is standard at the entry tier, and 24-hour turnaround with newsletter promotion exists at the premium tier.

### What is the difference between guest posting and sponsored posts?

Guest posts are editorial. They can sometimes be paid and sometimes be free. Sponsored posts are paid placements with a clear disclosure, often labeled "Sponsored" or "Partner content." Google treats both as legitimate if the disclosure is in place. The distinction matters legally under FTC and EU influencer rules, so make sure sponsored work is labeled as such.

### Can you buy guest posts on Google News sites?

Yes, several Google News-approved publications accept paid guest posts. The piece itself has to follow Google's news content policies: original reporting or analysis, a named author, no mass-produced PBN signals, and a clear disclosure. Many Google News sites reject paid placements outright, which is why the ones that do accept them hold the highest editorial bar.
