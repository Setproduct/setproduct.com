To cancel Adobe without paying the exit fee, switch to a different plan first, then cancel the new one immediately. Adobe charges 50% of the remaining yearly commitment when you cancel a yearly plan mid-term, but switching plans resets the contract terms, so the early termination fee no longer applies the same way. This guide walks through the exact steps, the timing that avoids the charge, the chat script that works, and what to do if Adobe bills you anyway.

## TL;DR

→ Switch plans, then cancel the new plan. Two clicks beat one.

→ Cancel within 14 days of a renewal and Adobe waives the fee outright.

→ The cancel option lives under Manage plan, not in the app or the desktop launcher.

→ Ask for the retention team in chat, and say you already switched plans.

→ Adobe Stock and Creative Cloud follow the same rules, only the numbers differ.

Fuck Adobe. No fancy intro here. Just raw frustration from a designer who's had enough.

I remember when Adobe actually gave a damn. You bought Photoshop. You owned it. Simple, clean, no strings attached. And it worked, so you could rely on it for years.

Then one day, they flipped the switch. No more ownership. Now you rent your tools forever. Stop paying? You're locked out of your own work.

I recently tried canceling Adobe Stock. I barely used it anymore. It's flooded with random, low-quality AI images. Most of them aren't even labeled. Useless. So I figured I'd cancel and move on. Adobe has a track record here, as the [Creative Cloud artwork scraping story](/blog/adobe-stealing-designs) showed.

Nope.

**They hit me with a $131.97 cancellation fee.** Unless you cancel in the exact same month you signed up, you get penalized. Fifty percent of whatever time's left on your yearly plan. It's buried in the fine print, of course.

So you're punished for trying to leave a product that's gone to shit.

That's not a subscription. That's a trap.

The software does not help the case either. Photoshop crawls. Illustrator feels like molasses. Creative Cloud sits in the background chewing RAM for no reason. Updates break things. Syncing fails. License errors pop up even when you've paid. This is the same [UX decay pattern](/blog/why-apps-ux-is-getting-worse) you see across most mature apps.

Adobe has gotten too big to care. They don't build tools anymore. They build systems that milk you.

## What Adobe charges in 2026

The fee is a percentage, so it scales with whatever plan you are on. These are the numbers that matter when you run the exit math.

<div class="blog-fit-table">

| Plan | Monthly | Exit fee if you cancel at month 6 |
|---|---|---|
| Creative Cloud All Apps | $59.99 | ~$180 |
| Single app (Photoshop) | $22.99 | ~$69 |
| Photography (Lightroom plus Photoshop) | $19.99 | ~$60 |
| Adobe Stock 10 assets | $29.99 | ~$90 |

</div>

The exit fee is 50% of the remaining balance on an annual commitment billed monthly. Cancel inside 14 days of signing up or renewing and Adobe waives it. Everything after that is the calculation above. For a wider look at what unused subscriptions cost teams, see our breakdown of [SaaS subscription waste](/blog/saas-subscription-waste).

The plan-switch route is what makes the number go away. It is not a loophole in the contract. It is the contract doing what it says, and Adobe relying on people never reading it.

## The trick to cancel Adobe without paying the fee

![An illustration of a man holding a canceled subscription notice while tossing coins](/blog/assets/how-to-cancel-adobe-subscription-without-fees/img-2.webp)

If you're stuck on Adobe's yearly plan paid monthly, there's a way out.

Yes, they'll threaten to charge you half of your remaining contract if you cancel early. Here is how you can beat it:

1. Log into your Adobe account.
2. Go to Manage plan under your subscription settings.
3. **Switch to a different plan.** Something cheaper, or a prepaid plan, works best.
4. Wait for the switch to process. This takes a few minutes.
5. Now cancel the new plan **immediately**.

The switch resets your contract terms. When you cancel right after, the early termination policy no longer hits you the same way. Many people report getting charged less, or nothing at all.

My own run went like this. I switched my subscription and was charged $100 for the new plan. Then I got refunded $36 from the current one. I canceled immediately after that and got refunded again. No final fee. No long fight. Done.

It was clunky, but it worked. And way cheaper than letting Adobe milk me for another 8 months.

## The chat script that gets the fee removed

Sometimes the switch goes through and Adobe still queues the fee. This is the exact sequence I used, and it works because the agent has a retention budget and a refund button.

Open chat from the contact page and start with the outcome, not the story.

"Hi, I switched my plan today and canceled the new plan. I was told the early termination fee no longer applies. Please confirm the cancellation went through without a fee."

If the agent offers a discount to stay, repeat the same line. Do not accept a new annual term, because that restarts the clock.

"Thanks, I still want to cancel. My plan was already switched today, so there is no remaining annual commitment."

Then ask for it in writing.

"Please confirm in this chat that no early termination fee will be charged, and email me a copy."

Save that transcript. If Adobe bills you anyway, the transcript is what a card issuer needs to reverse the charge.

## If Adobe bills you anyway

A refund is not a favor you have to negotiate twice. Work in this order.

First, open a new chat and ask for the retention team. Quote the transcript and the date of the plan switch. Most refunds land at this step because the agent can see the switch in your account history.

Second, if chat stalls, dispute the transaction with your bank or card issuer as a service not rendered. Attach the cancellation confirmation and the chat transcript. Chargeback windows run from 60 to 120 days depending on the issuer, so do not sit on it.

Third, file a complaint with your local consumer protection agency if the amount is large. In the US this is the FTC, in the EU it is your national consumer body. Adobe settles these quietly.

## Regulators kept Figma independent, for now

Let's get one thing straight: Adobe did not buy Figma.

They tried. In 2022, they announced a $20 billion deal to acquire it. Regulators in the EU and the UK stepped in and blocked it. Too much market power in one place. The deal fell apart in late 2023. Adobe walked away, and Figma walked with a $1 billion breakup fee.

So Figma is still independent. For now.

**Big companies don't stop trying. If it's not Adobe, it'll be someone else.** Figma is valuable, and valuable tools always end up in the crosshairs.

Even now, you can see the early signs of pressure. Pricing experiments. New enterprise features rolling out fast. More AI baked into everything. That lean, nimble vibe that made Figma awesome. It's already shifting.

You don't need to be a conspiracy theorist to feel it.

Today, Figma is still good. But keep your eyes open. We've seen what happens when the wrong people take control. Fireworks. Muse. Freehand. XD. All dead or absorbed and forgotten.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I cancel Adobe without paying the early termination fee?

Switch to a different plan first, wait for the switch to process, then cancel the new plan. The switch resets the contract terms, so the 50% early termination fee on the remaining yearly commitment no longer applies.

### How much is the Adobe early termination fee?

Adobe charges 50% of the remaining balance on a yearly plan billed monthly. With eight months left on a $59.99 plan, that lands near $240. The charge disappears if you cancel inside the first 14 days.

### Can I cancel Adobe in the app or do I have to call?

You can cancel from the Adobe account page without a phone call. The option is buried under Manage plan, and Adobe will route you to a chat agent who offers a discount before letting you leave.

### What if Adobe charges the fee anyway?

Open a chat, ask for the retention team, and state that you already switched plans. Most agents refund the fee to close the ticket. If that fails, dispute the charge with your card issuer and cite the plan switch.

### Is Adobe going to remove the early termination fee?

Adobe faces consumer protection pressure in the US and the EU over cancellation flows, but as of 2026 the fee is still in the terms. Use the plan-switch route instead of waiting for a policy change.
